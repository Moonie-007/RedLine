## Overview

Since the 1960s, NASA has left hardware across the solar system. When missions end, the machines stay behind, and most young space fans only hear about the spacecraft that are still working.

**Red Line** gives three of those machines a voice. On a late-1990s desktop, you open a chat app and talk to:

- **Ingenuity**, the tiny helicopter that was built for 5 flights and flew 72.
- **Spirit**, the gentle rover that climbed a hill and fell asleep in the sand.
- **Opportunity**, the stubborn rover built for 90 Martian days that kept driving for years.

Each robot tells its story as text messages, with real mission dates, jokes, a few tears and real photos from Mars. You reply, ask questions, and move the story forward. There are no quizzes and no walls of text. The aim is that you finish feeling a personal connection to a machine that is still sitting on Mars.

## How this meets the challenge

| Challenge goal | How EarthChat 98 responds |
| --- | --- |
| Teach school-age and casual space fans about hardware left on other worlds | Three missions, told by the machines themselves: a helicopter and the twin Mars Exploration Rovers |
| Explain what each machine is, why it was sent and what it discovered | Each story follows the same arc: landing, a challenge, a discovery, and an ending. Each message has an **(i)** button for a quick fact |
| Show how missions operated, rather than listing facts | You live the missions as they happen. The rover drags a broken wheel, rides out a dust storm and sends its last message, and you respond |
| Connect engineering to science | A broken wheel uncovers evidence of past water. Tiny iron-rich "blueberries" show Mars was once wet. A helicopter becomes a scout for a rover |
| Build a personal connection | First-person voices with humour and emotion, a "goodnight" for each rover, and profile pictures you can personalise |
| Use an accessible, widely available format | Runs in any modern web browser with no installation, accounts or dependencies |
| Strong narrative focus | The story is the game. Facts are optional and tucked behind **(i)** so the chat never overloads |

**Target audience:** ages 9 to 14 and casual space fans. Sentences are short and technical terms are explained in plain words.

## The hardware in the story

| Machine | Type | Where | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Ingenuity** | Helicopter, a technology demonstration | Jezero Crater, Mars | Last flight Jan 18, 2024. Mission ended Jan 25, 2024 after rotor damage. It remains on the surface |
| **Spirit** | Mars Exploration Rover | Gusev Crater, Mars | Last contact Mar 22, 2010. NASA ended recovery efforts May 25, 2011 |
| **Opportunity** | Mars Exploration Rover | Meridiani Planum, Mars | Last contact Jun 10, 2018, during a global dust storm. Mission declared complete Feb 13, 2019 |

Key facts used in the story: Ingenuity was planned as a 30-day, five-flight test and flew 72 times in almost three years. Spirit and Opportunity were designed for a 90-day mission, and Opportunity covered about 45 km before it fell silent.

## Features

- **Story told as text messages,** each with a mission date such as `[Feb 18, 2021]`.
- **Ask the robots anything.** Type a question and the rover answers in one or two short lines. Tap **(i)** for the longer explanation.
- **Spoiler-safe.** Each rover only knows its story up to the date it is currently at.
- **Mars photos in the chat.** Click a photo to enlarge it. All photos are also in the **Album** app.
- **Retro desktop.** A late-90s desktop with a sky-and-hill wallpaper, taskbar, Start menu and live clock, and screen grain and scanlines.
- **Early social-network chat layout** with an inbox, profile pictures and unread badges.
- **Cozy sound,** made live in the browser: soft chimes and a music-box tune, with a **Sound** toggle.
- **Make it yours.** Click any profile picture to change it. The Album has empty frames for your own images.

## How to play

1. Click the **Messaging** icon on the desktop (or use **Start**).
2. Pick a rover from the inbox.
3. Read its messages, then reply with a button or in your own words.
4. Press **Keep going, I’m listening** to hear the next moment.
5. Tap **(i)** for a quick fact. Type a question any time.
6. Explore the **Album**, **Recycle Bin** and the empty files on the desktop.

## Getting started

No install, build step or server is needed.

```bash
git clone <your-repo-url>
cd earthchat-project
# open index.html in a modern browser
```

Keep `index.html`, `style.css`, `script.js` and the `images/` folder together. An internet connection is only used to load the fonts. Without it, a standard console font is used.

## Customising

- **Story:** the rover messages, reactions, dates, facts and photos are in the `R` list at the top of `script.js`. Each beat has `d` (date), `m` (messages), `f` (the **(i)** fact), `im` (photos), `c` (reply buttons) and `re` (reaction).
- **Photos:** replace files in `images/` and keep the same names, or edit the `IMG` list in `script.js`.
- **Palette:** change the CSS variables at the top of `style.css`.
- **Add a rover:** copy a rover object in `R`, give it a new `id` and `name`, and add its profile picture to `PIC` and `IMG`.

## Accessibility

- Runs in the browser with no installation, on desktop and phone.
- Everything is readable text, with photos given alt text and the **(i)** facts available on demand.
- Buttons and inbox rows can be reached with the keyboard, with a visible focus outline.
- Sound is optional and can be switched off at any time. There is no flashing content, and the screen-grain animation is turned off for people who prefer reduced motion.
- Text uses high-contrast colours. The game also follows the device's dark mode.
- This has not been tested with a screen reader. Feedback is welcome.

## Accuracy and AI disclosure

- **Facts:** dates and mission facts come from NASA sources (see [Resources](#resources)). Landing dates use NASA's Pacific-time dates (Spirit Jan 3, Opportunity Jan 24, 2004). Some sources list one day later in UTC.
- **Voices:** the first-person lines, jokes and emotions are creative writing in the rovers' voices, not quotes. Opportunity's "low battery, getting dark" line is a poetic retelling of its final data, not an actual transmission.
- **Typed questions:** answers are generated by an AI model (Claude, through the claude.ai demo page). They are told to stay accurate and spoiler-safe, but can occasionally be wrong. The **(i)** facts and the story itself are fixed text and do not use AI.
- **Running locally:** typed questions get a "the signal wobbled, say that again?" reply, because the AI feature only exists on the demo page. Everything else works.
- **Photos:** Mars images are mission imagery. Check each image's credit and usage terms (for example NASA/JPL-Caltech) before publishing.

## Technology

Plain **HTML, CSS and JavaScript** with no frameworks or packages.

| Browser feature | Used for |
| --- | --- |
| Canvas 2D | The pixelated sky, clouds and hill wallpaper |
| Web Audio API | Chimes, blips and the music-box tune, with no sound files |
| Inline SVG | Pixel icons for the desktop |
| File inputs and `FileReader` | Changing profile pictures and adding your own photos |
| CSS variables, grid, flexbox, media queries | Layout, palette, dark mode and small screens |

Fonts: [Press Start 2P](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Press+Start+2P) and [VT323](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/VT323) from Google Fonts.
